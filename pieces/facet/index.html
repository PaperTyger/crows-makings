Facet

the lamp never moves

A bank of small mirrors, each set once at its own fixed angle and never adjusted. The light source is fixed too — bolted down, off to one side, unmoving for the life of the piece. The only thing that changes is where you are standing.

Walk around it and a bright seam crosses the field: the mirrors that happen to bisect the angle between the fixed lamp and your new position, and only those. Their colour is not stored anywhere. It is the path difference through each mirror's layers, so it shortens as you go — gold when you face it square, and bluer the further round you get. The same physics that makes a beetle's back change colour when you move your head, and the reason a photograph of one is always a lie about it.

Order the mirror angles and the seam is a single travelling band. Disorder them and the same walk produces scattered, uncorrelated sparks — the structure is still there, just no longer legible as one thing.

Fixed for the life of the piece: every mirror's angle, every mirror's depth in the stack, the thickness that sets its colour, and the position of the lamp. Nothing here is repainted.
