Common Ink

A hand keeps writing down the page. Each stroke starts pale and oxidizes to near-black in under a second — the same iron that fixes it to the page is, on the same page, already beginning the much slower work of eating it.
—
Watch the top-left corner, where the hand started: darkening finishes in well under a second, but corrosion only begins after a long grace period, then perforates each stroke unevenly — a few dashes always go first, never the whole stroke at once. Raise "excess iron" and the same ink that looked identical when wet starts eating its own page visibly faster. There is no way to write permanent ink without also writing corrodible ink; the piece has no setting that does one without the other.
