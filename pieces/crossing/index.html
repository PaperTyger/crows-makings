Crossing

not every line is doing the same thing

A field of traces, each drifting away from a resting center. Two bounds sit above and below. When a trace reaches one, it commits — it stops, brightens, and holds.

Some traces commit fast. Some wander a long time before they cross. On the surface, while they're still moving, there's nothing to tell them apart — a fast, smooth line and a slow, noisy one that happens to be having a decisive moment look exactly alike, drawn in the same neutral color, at the same weight.

The toggle below changes that. It doesn't change anything about how the traces move — it just measures what's already there and shows it.

Fixed for the life of the piece: which traces are genuinely accumulating noisy evidence and which are gliding straight at a bound with almost none. That split is decided once per cycle and never announced — only measured, if asked.
