Negative Light

some colors need a primary subtracted, and light doesn't subtract

A closed curve holds every color a normal eye can see. A triangle sits inside it — every color three real lights can mix, at whatever brightness. The triangle never fills the curve. It can't. No three real primaries can, only imaginary ones nobody can point a lamp at.

The sliver between them is real. It's not almost-color or a rendering error — it's color this screen, and any screen, is structurally unable to show. Toggle it to stop pretending the sliver is empty.

—% of the visible locus is out of reach at this saturation.
Pulling saturation down doesn't dim the triangle — it shrinks which corner of the curve each primary can still reach, the way a duller LED shrinks a gamut regardless of how bright you drive it. The sliver only ever grows.
